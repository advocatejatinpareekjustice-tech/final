Advocate Jatin Pareek handles all categories of civil litigation before Jaipur District & Sessions Courts and the Rajasthan High Court. This includes ancestral property partition suits, temporary injunctions (stay orders against unauthorized construction or alienation), suits for declaration of title, specific performance of contract, recovery suits, and execution of court decrees.

The timeframe varies depending on the relief sought. Urgent applications for stay orders (temporary injunctions under Order 39 CPC) are typically argued and decided in weeks to a few months. Complete trials for partition or declaration can take 1 to 3 years. Advocate Jatin Pareek emphasizes proactive filings and evidence preparation to prevent unnecessary adjournments.

Mutation (नामान्तरण / दाखिल खारिज) is the legal entry in revenue records (Khasra & Jamabandi) recording the transfer of land ownership following a sale, gift, or inheritance. Without mutation, the official government revenue ledger continues to reflect the seller or deceased ancestor, preventing legitimate re-sale, bank loans, and patta issuance.

Revenue court orders in Rajasthan follow a statutory appeal hierarchy: an order of a Tehsildar can be appealed before the Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO), an SDO order before the Revenue Appellate Authority (RAA) or District Collector, and higher revisions/appeals lie before the Board of Revenue for Rajasthan at Ajmer and the Rajasthan High Court.

Yes. Under Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908, registration of a Sale Deed or Gift Deed for immovable property is legally compulsory. An unregistered document does not confer valid title in law and cannot be produced as proof of ownership in a court of law.

An Agreement to Sell (इकरारनामा) is a preliminary contract outlining terms, sale price, and future closing conditions — it creates a contractual obligation to sell but does not transfer property title. A registered Sale Deed (बैनामा) is the final, stamped deed executed before the Sub-Registrar that officially transfers complete title and ownership to the buyer.

An unregistered Will is valid under Indian law provided it is signed by the testator in the presence of two witnesses. However, registering the Will with the Sub-Registrar in Jaipur is strongly recommended. A registered Will drastically reduces the possibility of false allegations of forgery, coercion, or mental illness by disgruntled heirs.

Revenue record corrections — including Khasra correction, Khatauni correction, and Jamabandi correction — require filing a formal application before the Tehsildar or SDO Revenue Court.
